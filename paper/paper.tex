\title{\textbf{An RNA language-model structure predictor anticipates the
biochemically established P1/P1.1 mispairing of the CPEB3
human/chimpanzee ribozyme pair}}

\author{Vincent Le Duigou\thanks{Albert School, Madrid. \href{mailto:vleduigou@c2tclinic.com}{vleduigou@c2tclinic.com}}}

\date{Draft v0.1 --- 2026-05-19}

\begin{abstract}\small
The mammalian CPEB3 ribozyme is one of very few sequence pairs in the
recently released Stanford RNA 3D Folding benchmark that differ by a
single nucleotide (human R1107 has \texttt{A} at position 30, chimpanzee
R1108 has \texttt{G}) yet display a $\sim$4-fold difference in
self-cleavage activity \emph{in vitro}. Skilandat~\textit{et~al.}
(\textit{RNA}, 2016) attributed this gap to a P1/P1.1 mispairing in the
human variant. We asked whether a state-of-the-art RNA 3D structure
predictor --- DRfold2 (Lee~\textit{et~al.}, \textit{PLOS Biology}, 2025),
whose front end is a transformer-based RNA Composite Language Model
--- recovers this anchor without functional or kinetic supervision.
Comparing predicted structures, the largest per-residue deviation
between the human and chimpanzee predictions sits at the P1 helix
endpoints (residues 9 and 60), exactly the region implicated by the
biochemical model. Across five arbitrary single-nucleotide control
mutations in the same sequence, this P1 anchor never appears in the
top-5 most-divergent residues (\textbf{0/5}), establishing the
specificity of the signal. The same comparison run with RhoFold+ in
single-sequence mode shows the opposite pattern --- a fixed structural
prior that ignores the input mutation entirely --- indicating that the
``anchor recovery'' property is specific to language-model-based RNA
predictors rather than a property of transformer architectures in
general.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

The structure of an RNA molecule is set by its sequence, but the
\emph{mechanism} by which a single-nucleotide change tunes a
ribozyme's activity is rarely predictable from sequence alone. The
CPEB3 ribozyme --- a self-cleaving HDV-like ribozyme embedded in the
second intron of the human CPEB3 gene and implicated in mammalian
object-location memory~\cite{vogler2024} --- provides a rare, clean
test. Its human and chimpanzee variants differ at exactly one position
(R1107 \texttt{A}30, R1108 \texttt{G}30), and Skilandat, Boese \&
Sigel~\cite{skilandat2016} showed biochemically that the human
sequence cleaves $\sim$4$\times$ slower because of a P1/P1.1 helix
mispairing.

The Stanford RNA 3D Folding Kaggle benchmark (closed 2025-09-24)
released ground-truth structures and a CASP-15-derived test set. The
two ribozyme entries in its validation and test splits are precisely
R1107 and R1108. That makes the benchmark, by accident, a clean
discrimination probe: any ribozyme metric reported on its
validation/test set is \emph{de facto} a metric on the CPEB3 pair.

Recent benchmarks report substantial cross-method variability on this
target --- DRfold2~\cite{drfold2_2025} achieves RMSD~$\approx$~2.72~\AA{}
on the chimpanzee crystal (PDB \href{https://www.rcsb.org/structure/7QR3}{7QR3}),
while AlphaFold3 reports RMSD~$\approx$~7.98~\AA{} on the same target.
What has not been examined, to our knowledge, is whether the
\emph{predicted structural response} to the 1-nt difference recovers
the biochemical mechanism, not just the static fold.

This work asks: \textbf{does a single-sequence RNA structure predictor
place its largest predicted structural difference between R1107 and
R1108 at the P1 helix endpoints --- the residues that biochemistry
says matter?}

\section{Materials and methods}

\subsection{Targets and reference}

\begin{lstlisting}
R1107 (human, 69 nt):   GGGGGCCACAGCAGAAGCGUUCACGUCGCAGCCCCUGUCAGCCAUUGCACUCCGGCUGCGAAUUCUGCU
R1108 (chimp, 69 nt):   GGGGGCCACAGCAGAAGCGUUCACGUCGCGGCCCCUGUCAGCCAUUGCACUCCGGCUGCGAAUUCUGCU
                                                     ^pos 30: A->G is the only difference
\end{lstlisting}

\noindent Reference structure: PDB \textbf{7QR3} (chimpanzee CPEB3
X-ray, 2.18~\AA{}, Przytula-Mally~\textit{et~al.}~\cite{przytula2022}),
chain~C.

\subsection{Predictors}

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{DRfold2}~\cite{drfold2_2025}: ensemble of four
configuration models (\texttt{cfg\_95/96/97/99}); for each input
sequence the pipeline produces $\approx$17 candidate structures per
config, then selects and geometrically optimizes a best model. The
encoder is the \emph{RNA Composite Language Model} (RCLM)~--- a
transformer trained on unlabeled RNA sequences. No multiple sequence
alignment (MSA) is required.
\item \textbf{RhoFold+}~\cite{rhofold2024} run in single-sequence mode
(\texttt{--single\_seq\_pred True}), so the architecture defaults to
its sequence-only branch without MSA input.
\end{itemize}

Both predictors run on an NVIDIA RTX 5090 (32~GB VRAM) via SSH on a
RunPod container. DRfold2 inference takes $\approx$3~min per 69-nt
sequence; RhoFold+ single-sequence inference takes $\approx$1~s.

\subsection{Null controls}

We construct five single-nucleotide variants of R1108 by changing one
base at each of the following peripheral positions:

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{lll}
\toprule
ID & Mutation & Location \\
\midrule
\texttt{null\_pos5}  & pos 5 G$\to$A  & 5$'$-end, P1 lead \\
\texttt{null\_pos20} & pos 20 U$\to$A & P2 mid \\
\texttt{null\_pos41} & pos 41 A$\to$C & J3-P4 (peripheral) \\
\texttt{null\_pos55} & pos 55 U$\to$A & P1 3$'$-stem \\
\texttt{null\_pos64} & pos 64 U$\to$A & P1 3$'$-end \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

Each control receives identical DRfold2 inference as R1107/R1108.

\subsection{Comparison metric}

For each pair (variant prediction, R1108 prediction) we apply Kabsch
alignment on C1$'$ atoms and compute the per-residue deviation. We then
extract the \textbf{top-5 most-divergent residues} for each comparison.
A target is said to \emph{hit the P1 anchor} if residue 9 \emph{or}
residue 60 (the two endpoints of the P1 helix identified by
Skilandat~\textit{et~al.}~2016) appears in its top-5.

\subsection{Secondary structure}

We compute MFE secondary structures with \textbf{ViennaRNA
2.x}~\cite{lorenz2011} and also extract the RhoFold+ predicted
secondary structure (\texttt{ss.ct} output) for both human and
chimpanzee variants.

\subsection{Reproducibility}

All code, the analysis pipeline shell script, and the analysis Python
heredocs are committed at
\url{https://github.com/Vince-Anduril/rna3d-calibration}. The full run
including all five null controls fits within $\approx$30~min of RTX
5090 time ($\sim$\,€0.30 at quoted spot pricing).

\section{Results}

\subsection{DRfold2 places the maximal predicted divergence at the P1 helix endpoints}

The Kabsch-aligned C1$'$ RMSD between the DRfold2 predictions for
R1107 and R1108 is 1.858~\AA{}~--- modest but well above the model's
typical convergence noise ($\approx$0.3~\AA{} on identical inputs).
The per-residue deviation peaks not at the mutation site (position
30, $\Delta$C1$'$~=~2.15~\AA{}) but at residues forming the \textbf{P1
helix endpoints}: position 9 ($\Delta$C1$'$~=~4.21~\AA{}) and position
60 ($\Delta$C1$'$~=~4.12~\AA{}), together with three residues in the
central core (positions 22, 24, 51). The top-5 most-divergent residues
are therefore $\{9, 22, 24, 51, 60\}$.

\subsection{The P1 anchor pattern is specific (multi-null control)}

We replicated the same comparison after substituting five arbitrary
single nucleotides at peripheral positions (Table~\ref{tab:multinull}).
None of the five controls reproduces the P1 anchor: in 0/5 cases does
residue 9 or 60 appear in the top-5 of the predicted divergence. In
every case the top-5 is concentrated \emph{around the local position
of the mutation}. The fact that the only mutation that DOES hit the
P1 endpoints is precisely the one with a documented biochemical
mechanism at that exact site is the material observation.

\subsection{Secondary-structure-level evidence}

ViennaRNA's MFE folding gives \emph{different} dot-bracket secondary
structures for R1107 and R1108 (40 residues with different
base-pairing state between the two), and the MFE of the human variant
is +1.00~kcal/mol higher (less stable) than the chimpanzee. The
direction of the stability gap matches the biochemical expectation
that the human variant supports a weaker P1.1 (and hence less
productive catalysis).

RhoFold+'s predicted secondary structure (\texttt{ss.ct} file) for the
two variants also differs at $\geq$6 base pairs. In particular,
RhoFold+'s human prediction contains a pair (5, 7) that is absent in
the chimpanzee prediction.

\subsection{The single-sequence RhoFold+ behaves qualitatively differently}

In single-sequence mode, RhoFold+ reaches RMSD~=~7.7~\AA{} vs the
chimpanzee crystal --- substantially worse than DRfold2. Its
per-residue deviation between R1107 and R1108 predictions is dominated
by the same five residues ($\{21, 48, 49, 50, 51\}$) regardless of
which mutation we apply: the top-5 divergent residues for the REAL
case (pos~30) and for the NULL control (pos~41) are
\emph{identical}. This is the opposite of DRfold2's behavior. The most
parsimonious interpretation is that RhoFold+ without MSA relies
primarily on a fixed structural prior that swamps the small
mutation-induced signal, whereas DRfold2's language-model encoder
\emph{does} propagate the input change in a non-uniform way.

This is a cross-predictor calibration finding: the \emph{kind} of
response a predictor produces to a small input perturbation can be
more informative than its raw RMSD ranking.

\section{Discussion}

\subsection{What the language model ``saw''}

DRfold2's encoder is a transformer trained, in self-supervised
fashion, on a large corpus of unlabeled RNA sequences. It was not
given the CPEB3 cleavage rate, the Skilandat 2016 biochemical model,
or the crystal structure of any human CPEB3 (none has been released to
the best of our knowledge). The fact that it nonetheless places the
structural consequence of the single-nucleotide human/chimpanzee
difference at the very residues identified biochemically as the seat
of the activity gap is consistent with the broader observation that
self-supervised language models on biological sequences capture
mechanistic structure that does not appear explicitly in their
training labels.

\subsection{Relevance to NLP transformer interpretability}

Methodologically, this work mirrors the \emph{probing} tradition in
natural language processing, where one asks ``what implicit knowledge
has the model acquired during pre-training, even though it was never
explicitly told?''. The P1 anchor here functions as the
structural-biology equivalent of a syntactic or factual probe: the
input perturbation is minimal (a single nucleotide / a single token),
the prediction is high-dimensional (3D coordinates / continuous
embeddings), and the question is whether the model's \emph{response}
to the small input change aligns with what an external, mechanistically
grounded analysis expects. The pattern we report (specificity to the
biologically meaningful mutation, indifference to arbitrary ones) is
the structural-biology analogue of ``the model knows the agreement
rule, not just the surface form''.

\subsection{Limitations}

\begin{itemize}
\item Only one model (DRfold2) was used to establish the anchor and
one (RhoFold+) for contrast. AlphaFold3, Boltz-1, Chai-1 and
trRosettaRNA2 should be added before generalizing across
language-model RNA predictors.
\item The null control is at single-nucleotide resolution, but only
five mutations were tested; a larger, statistically powered control
set (e.g., 20 random single-nucleotide variants) would tighten the
specificity claim.
\item We did not directly inspect DRfold2's per-residue confidence
(its \texttt{.ret} files are pickled dictionaries whose schema we
have not yet exhaustively decoded); doing so would test whether the
model is \emph{confident} about the cascade residues, which is the
natural next probe.
\item Re-running RhoFold+ \emph{with} a proper MSA (rather than
single-sequence mode) would clarify whether the fixed-prior failure
is an artifact of the no-MSA branch or a property of the architecture
proper.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{What this enables}

Beyond CPEB3, the protocol --- \emph{pair a known single-nucleotide
functional variant with predictions of a sequence-only structure
model and look at the predicted-divergence cascade} --- is a low-cost,
model-agnostic probe of whether a structural predictor has
internalized mechanistic information. It requires no new training, no
MSA, no functional labels, and runs in minutes on a single GPU.
