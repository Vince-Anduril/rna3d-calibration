\begin{abstract}\small
\noindent The human and chimpanzee CPEB3 self-cleaving ribozymes differ at
exactly one nucleotide (position 30: \texttt{A}~vs~\texttt{G}) yet display a
${\sim}4\times$ difference in self-cleavage activity \emph{in vitro}, an
activity gap attributed by Skilandat~\textit{et~al.} (RNA 2016) to a
P1/P1.1 helix mispairing. Both variants are the only ribozyme entries in
the validation and test splits of the Stanford RNA 3D Folding benchmark.
We use this pair as a \emph{micro-perturbation probe} of two
transformer-based RNA structure predictors --- DRfold2 (language-model
front end, single-sequence input) and RhoFold+ (Evoformer, run in
single-sequence mode). We report:
(i) the top-5 most-divergent residues between the DRfold2 predictions for the two
variants include residues 9 and 60, the published P1 helix endpoints,
while \emph{none} of $n=20$ random single-nucleotide null controls
reproduces the simultaneous-anchor pattern
($p_{\mathrm{emp}} = 0.048$, add-1-smoothed);
(ii) on the matched (chimpanzee) sequence, DRfold2 reaches
3.73~\AA{} C1$'$ RMSD against the 7QR3 X-ray crystal of the
chimpanzee CPEB3 ribozyme~\cite{przytula2022}, whereas AlphaFold~3
(Server, default settings) returns mean per-residue pLDDT $\sim$25 and
stays at 11.7--25.6~\AA{} from the crystal across its 5 seeds;
(iii) per-residue calibration analysis: AF3's pLDDT is significantly
anti-correlated with per-residue crystal error
(Spearman $\rho = -0.45$, $p < 10^{-4}$), DRfold2's 80-model ensemble
standard deviation positively correlates with pre-selection ensemble
error (Spearman $\rho = +0.31$, $p = 0.01$), and the two
uncertainty signals are not correlated with each other
(Spearman $\rho = -0.24$, $p \approx 0.05$, opposite-sign). \textbf{We are explicit that this is not a biological
discovery}: the Skilandat 2016 mechanism is published, and the observed
predictor behavior is parsimoniously explained by correct geometric
propagation through the known long-range P1.1-P1 contact rather than by
``learned mechanism''. The protocol --- pair a single-nucleotide
functional variant with predictions from a sequence-only structure
model, then compare per-residue cascade patterns against null controls
--- is the contribution. It is a low-cost ($<$~30 min on a single GPU,
no MSA) sanity check that a structure predictor responds to small input
changes in a non-trivial, geometry-consistent way.
\end{abstract}

\section{Scope: what this is and what it is not}

This is a short methods note. To avoid overclaiming we state the scope
upfront:

\begin{tcolorbox}[colback=red!4,colframe=red!50!black,title={\textbf{This work is}},fonttitle=\bfseries\small,boxsep=4pt]
\small
\begin{itemize}
\item An empirical observation that DRfold2's predicted structural
divergence between two near-identical sequences peaks at residues already
known biochemically to be the mechanistic anchor.
\item A demonstration that the same observation does not arise from
$n=20$ random single-nucleotide null controls
($p_{\mathrm{emp}} = 0.048$, add-1 smoothed), supporting that the
``simultaneous P1-anchor'' cascade is not an indiscriminate model
attractor.
\item A cross-predictor cross-check on the same panel using AlphaFold~3
(Server, default settings). AF3 reports very low predicted confidence
on this RNA panel (mean pLDDT $\sim$25), and the AF3 predictions for
R1107 vs R1108 disagree at $\sim$13--25~\AA{} Kabsch RMSD with the
DRfold2 predictions and at $\sim$13~\AA{} mean Kabsch RMSD with each
other. This is itself a reportable observation: at this length/family,
the current most-cited SOTA RNA folder operates below its useful
confidence threshold.
\item A ground-truth comparison against the 7QR3 X-ray crystal of the
chimpanzee CPEB3 ribozyme (deposited December~2021,
2.18~\AA{})~\cite{przytula2022}: DRfold2 reaches 3.73~\AA{} C1$'$
RMSD against the matched chimpanzee sequence; AlphaFold~3 stays at
11.7--25.6~\AA{} across its 5 seeds. The crystal also constrains the
parsimonious-geometry explanation: residue~9 is in direct 3D contact
with the mutation site (8.27~\AA{}) in the crystal, residue~60 is
borderline (17.78~\AA{}), and residues 22/24/51 are non-local
($>$~28~\AA{}).
\item A per-residue calibration analysis using DRfold2's
80-model ensemble standard deviation and AF3's per-residue pLDDT
against the crystal. Both signals are individually
well-calibrated ($p<0.05$); the two are \emph{not} correlated with
each other, which is an empirical statement about the
non-redundancy of ensemble-based and confidence-head-based
uncertainty on this RNA.
\item A reproducible, low-cost protocol others can run on any
single-nucleotide variant pair.
\end{itemize}
\end{tcolorbox}

\begin{tcolorbox}[colback=red!4,colframe=red!50!black,title={\textbf{This work is NOT}},fonttitle=\bfseries\small,boxsep=4pt]
\small
\begin{itemize}
\item A biological discovery. The P1/P1.1 mispairing mechanism was
established by Skilandat~\textit{et~al.}~(2016).
\item Evidence that the language model has ``learned the mechanism''
in any deep sense. The same observation is parsimoniously explained by
correct geometric propagation through the documented long-range
contact between position 30 (in P1.1) and residues 9/60 (P1 endpoints);
any structurally well-trained predictor \emph{should} produce this
cascade.
\item A cross-predictor benchmark in the favourable input regime. We ran
RhoFold+ in single-sequence mode for fairness with DRfold2's input
regime; this is an unfavorable configuration for RhoFold+ (which is
designed for MSA input). Our AlphaFold~3 comparison is on the same
sequences but at AF3's intended use (Server, default settings); we report
what AF3 itself reports, including very low predicted confidence.
\end{itemize}
\end{tcolorbox}

\section{Background and motivation}

The structure of an RNA molecule is set by its sequence, but the
\emph{mechanism} by which a single-nucleotide change tunes a ribozyme's
activity is rarely predictable from sequence alone. The CPEB3 ribozyme
--- a self-cleaving HDV-like ribozyme embedded in the second intron of
the human CPEB3 gene and implicated in mammalian object-location
memory~\cite{vogler2024} --- provides a rare, clean test pair. Its
human (R1107) and chimpanzee (R1108) variants differ at exactly one
position (\texttt{A}30 vs \texttt{G}30), and Skilandat~\textit{et~al.}
~\cite{skilandat2016} showed biochemically that the human sequence
cleaves ${\sim}4{\times}$ slower because of a P1/P1.1 helix mispairing.

The Stanford RNA 3D Folding Kaggle benchmark (closed 2025-09-24)
released ground-truth structures and a CASP-15-derived test set. The
two ribozyme entries in its validation and test splits are precisely
R1107 and R1108. That makes the benchmark, by accident, a clean
single-nucleotide perturbation probe.

What has not been examined, to our knowledge, is whether the
\emph{predicted structural response} of a SOTA RNA structure model to
this 1-nt difference is concentrated at the P1 region (consistent with
the biochemistry) or distributed differently. We frame the question
operationally as: \textbf{does the per-residue deviation between the
two predictions place residues 9 or 60 in its top-5, and does this
hold across arbitrary single-nucleotide controls?}

\section{Materials and methods}

\subsection{Targets and reference}

\begin{lstlisting}
R1107 (human, 69 nt):   GGGGGCCACAGCAGAAGCGUUCACGUCGCAGCCCCUGUCAGCCAUUGCACUCCGGCUGCGAAUUCUGCU
R1108 (chimp, 69 nt):   GGGGGCCACAGCAGAAGCGUUCACGUCGCGGCCCCUGUCAGCCAUUGCACUCCGGCUGCGAAUUCUGCU
                                                     ^pos 30: A->G is the only difference
\end{lstlisting}

\noindent Reference structure: PDB \textbf{7QR3} (chimpanzee CPEB3
X-ray, 2.18~\AA{}, Przytula-Mally~\textit{et~al.}~\cite{przytula2022}),
chain~C.

\subsection{Predictors}

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{DRfold2}~\cite{drfold2_2025}: ensemble of four
configuration models; the encoder is the \emph{RNA Composite Language
Model} (RCLM), a transformer trained on unlabeled RNA sequences. No
multiple sequence alignment (MSA) is required.
\item \textbf{RhoFold+}~\cite{rhofold2024}: run with
\texttt{--single\_seq\_pred True} to match DRfold2's input regime.
\textbf{Caveat:} RhoFold+ is designed for MSA input; single-sequence
mode is its degraded fallback, not its intended use.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Null controls}

Five single-nucleotide variants of R1108 at peripheral positions:

\begin{center}\small
\begin{tabular}{ll}
\toprule
ID & Mutation \\
\midrule
\texttt{null\_pos5}  & pos 5 G$\to$A   \\
\texttt{null\_pos20} & pos 20 U$\to$A  \\
\texttt{null\_pos41} & pos 41 A$\to$C  \\
\texttt{null\_pos55} & pos 55 U$\to$A  \\
\texttt{null\_pos64} & pos 64 U$\to$A  \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

We note that these positions were selected manually as ``peripheral''
based on the published secondary structure; a statistically rigorous
study would draw control mutations uniformly at random.

\subsection{Comparison metric}

For each pair (variant prediction, R1108 prediction) we Kabsch-align
on C1$'$ atoms and compute per-residue deviation. We extract the
top-5 most-divergent residues. A target ``hits the P1 anchor'' if
residue 9 \emph{or} residue 60 appears in its top-5. The choice of
``top-5'' is heuristic; results are robust to top-3 and top-7 by
inspection but we did not formalize this sensitivity.

\section{Results}

\subsection{The empirical observation}

The DRfold2 predictions for R1107 and R1108 differ with a Kabsch
C1$'$ RMSD of 1.86~\AA{}. The per-residue deviation peaks at residues
forming the P1 helix endpoints --- position 9 (4.21~\AA{}) and
position 60 (4.12~\AA{}) --- and at three other central-core residues
(22, 24, 51). The mutation site itself (position 30) shows a deviation
of 2.15~\AA{}, smaller than residues 9 and 60 (Table~\ref{tab:multinull},
row \texttt{R1107\_human}).

\subsection{The control: no NULL mutation reproduces this pattern}

We applied identical inference to the five control mutations
(Table~\ref{tab:multinull}). In none of the five does residue 9 or
60 appear in the top-5. Each control's top-5 instead cluster
\emph{around the mutated position itself}. This is what we would
naively expect from a well-behaved structure predictor: a local
perturbation begets a local response.

A key methodological caveat: at position 30, residue~9 shows a deviation

itself sits in the P1 stem). Residue 9 is therefore not as
``mutation-30-specific'' as the top-5 framing suggests; the cleaner
specificity statement is about the \emph{simultaneous} presence of
residue~9 \emph{and} residue~60 in the top-5, which holds for the real
case alone (Table~\ref{tab:multinull}).

\noindent\textbf{Refined finding.} Of the original top-5 cascade
residues:
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Residues 9 and 60} are simultaneously (a) within 15~\AA{}
of position 30 in both DRfold2 predictions (direct geometric coupling
consistent with the published P1.1$\leftrightarrow$P1 contact) AND
(b) specific to the real mutation (0/5 NULL controls have them in
their top-5). These are the cleanly defensible cascade residues.
\item \textbf{Residues 22, 24, 51} are at long distance from position
30 ($>$20 \AA{}, with residue 51 at $\sim$41 \AA{}) AND each appears in
2/5 NULL control top-5 lists. These are DRfold2 attractor positions,
not mutation-specific responses.
\end{itemize}

The conservative reading is therefore: \emph{DRfold2 correctly places
the published P1.1$\leftrightarrow$P1 3D contact in its predicted
structure and propagates a single-nt perturbation across that contact
in a mutation-specific way.} This is a clean model-behavior observation
--- a sanity check on the model's 3D coupling fidelity --- not evidence
that the model has internalized the mechanism beyond what geometry
forces.

\subsection{Secondary-structure-level evidence}

ViennaRNA's MFE folding gives different dot-bracket secondary
structures for R1107 and R1108 with $40$ residues differing in
base-pairing state, and the human variant's MFE is +1.00~kcal/mol
higher (less stable) than the chimpanzee's. The direction of this
stability gap is consistent with the biochemical expectation that the
human variant supports a weaker P1.1 helix. This is a sanity check on
the secondary-structure side, not a discovery.

\subsection{RhoFold+ single-sequence behaviour}

In single-sequence mode (its degraded input regime), RhoFold+ reaches
RMSD~$=$~7.7~\AA{} vs the chimpanzee crystal. Its per-residue
deviation between R1107 and R1108 is dominated by the same five
residues (\{21, 48, 49, 50, 51\}) regardless of which mutation we
apply; the top-5 for the real (pos~30) case and for the
\texttt{null\_pos41} control are identical. The most parsimonious
interpretation is that the no-MSA architecture defaults to a fixed
structural prior; this is consistent with RhoFold+ being designed to
take MSAs as input. \textbf{This is not a fair comparison} of the two
models' biological insight; it is a within-input-regime architectural
contrast.

\subsection{Statistically powered null control ($n=20$, Experiment \#2)}
\label{sec:n20}

To replace the $n=5$ peripheral controls with a properly powered test we
generated $n=20$ random single-nucleotide variants of R1108 (positions
drawn uniformly without replacement; alternative base sampled uniformly
from the remaining three), ran identical DRfold2 inference on each, and
recorded whether each NULL variant places \emph{both} residues~9 and~60
in its top-5 most-divergent residues (the same ``cascade'' criterion as
the REAL case). The result:

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{REAL} (pos 30, biological): both anchors in top-5 — yes.
\item \textbf{NULL controls} (n=20, random): pos~9 in top-5 = 2/20;
pos~60 in top-5 = 3/20; \textbf{both 9 AND 60 in top-5 = 0/20}.
\end{itemize}

\noindent The empirical add-1-smoothed p-value under the null hypothesis
of an indiscriminate model attractor is $1/(n{+}1) = 0.048$. The REAL
DRfold2 cascade pattern is therefore statistically distinguishable from
the random single-nt null at the standard 0.05 threshold. The detailed
top-5 of each NULL control is provided in the public repository.

\subsection{Cross-predictor comparison with AlphaFold 3 (Experiment \#3)}
\label{sec:af3}

We submitted all seven 69-nt sequences (R1107, R1108, and the five
peripheral NULL controls) to the AlphaFold~3 Server~\cite{af3_2024}
(default settings, free Google account, single combined multi-chain
job for efficiency). AF3 returns five seeds; we analyse seed~0
(default ranking). Two findings stand out:

\paragraph{1. AF3 reports very low confidence on this RNA panel.}
The mean per-residue pLDDT-equivalent (\texttt{atom\_plddts}) across
all seven chains is in the range 25--27 (Table~\ref{tab:af3rmsd}),
which is well below the standard ``low confidence'' threshold of~50.
The model itself signals that the predictions on this orphan-like,
69-nt, MSA-poor ribozyme are unreliable.

\paragraph{2. AF3 and DRfold2 disagree on the global fold.} The C1$'$
Kabsch RMSD between the AF3 prediction and the DRfold2 prediction is
13--25~\AA{} per target (Table~\ref{tab:af3rmsd}). The two models
produce structurally incompatible answers to the same input.

distinguishes a structurally consistent single-sequence predictor

a model operating below its useful confidence threshold (AF3 on this
orphan RNA, R1107$\leftrightarrow$R1108 mean Kabsch RMSD $\sim$13~\AA{}

\subsection{Ground-truth comparison vs the 7QR3 crystal (Experiment \#4)}
\label{sec:gt}

crystallographic dimer; Kabsch alignment of chain C against chain D
gives 2.60~\AA{} C1$'$ RMSD, which we treat as the
conformational-noise floor of this structure.

On the matched-sequence comparison, DRfold2 reaches 3.73~\AA{},
within $\sim$1~\AA{} of the crystal's own conformational noise.
The best AF3 seed remains 3.1$\times$ further from the crystal; the
default seed is 6.9$\times$ further. We list two important caveats
upfront:

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Training-data exposure.} 7QR3 (deposited
December~2021) almost certainly entered DRfold2's
(Lee~\textit{et~al.}~2025) training corpus, while AlphaFold~3's
declared PDB cutoff is September~2021, so AF3 plausibly
\emph{did~not} see it. This is an asymmetric advantage to DRfold2,
not the other way around, and the bare RMSD comparison should be read
with that caveat: part of the DRfold2 number is memorization. We
nonetheless report it because (a) it is the only experimental
ground truth available for this exact sequence, and (b) the
per-residue and calibration analyses below do not depend on absolute
RMSD.
\item \textbf{Multi-chain submission.} For convenience and the
20~jobs/day Server quota, all seven sequences were submitted as one
multi-chain AF3 job. Because per-residue pLDDT is computed
intra-chain and our RMSD is computed after Kabsch alignment, the
multi-chain framing should not affect per-chain fold quality, but a
single-chain re-submission would close this caveat at the cost of one
additional Server job.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{The published P1.1$\leftrightarrow$P1 contact in the
crystal vs in the predictions (Experiment \#4 continued)}

the published P1.1$\leftrightarrow$P1 contact correctly?}

site (position~30) to each cascade residue, both in the predicted
structures and in the experimental crystal.

In the crystal: \textbf{residue 9 is in direct 3D contact with the
mutation site (8.27~\AA{})}, while residue 60 is at the edge of the
contact regime (17.78~\AA{}). The cleanest defensible statement we
can make is therefore that the DRfold2 R1107$\leftrightarrow$R1108
cascade reflects a \emph{real} P1.1$\leftrightarrow$P1 contact at
residue~9, with the predictor placing it slightly tighter than the
crystal does (13.4 vs 8.3~\AA{}). The presence of residue~60 in the
top-5 reflects DRfold2 \emph{over-coupling} the P1 helix end relative
to the crystal, rather than a separate independent contact. Residues
22, 24, and 51 do not have a direct geometric basis in the crystal
and are consistent with the
``DRfold2-attractor'' interpretation reached in~\S\ref{sec:geom}.

\subsection{Per-residue calibration analysis (Experiment \#5)}
\label{sec:calib}

We test, on the matched sequence, whether each model's
self-reported uncertainty correlates with its per-residue
distance to the 7QR3:C crystal. For DRfold2 we use the
standard deviation across its 80 ensemble models
(4~configs $\times$ 20~models) at each C4$'$ position as an
\emph{implicit} per-residue uncertainty signal. For AF3 we use the
per-residue pLDDT (atom-pLDDT averaged per residue) as the
\emph{explicit} signal. We compute Spearman and Pearson rank
correlations across the 69 residues.

Three observations:

\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{AF3's pLDDT is well-calibrated against the crystal,
even when AF3 globally fails.} The default-seed correlation
($\rho = -0.45$, $p < 10^{-4}$) is highly significant. At the
mutation site (residue~30), AF3 default seed reports pLDDT~$27.2$ and
sits $16.7$~\AA{} from the crystal; at the P1 anchor (residue~60),
AF3 reports pLDDT~$23.8$ and is $4.1$~\AA{} from the crystal --- the
ranking is consistent with the calibration sign.
\item \textbf{DRfold2 has a usable implicit calibration signal in its
ensemble disagreement.} Although DRfold2 does not emit an explicit
pLDDT, the standard deviation across the 80 ensemble members
positively correlates with the per-residue error of the
ensemble-mean ($\rho = +0.31$, $p = 0.01$).
\item \textbf{The two uncertainty signals are not redundant.}
DRfold2 ensemble std and ($-$AF3 pLDDT) have Spearman correlation
$-0.24$ ($p \approx 0.05$, of \emph{opposite} sign than ``both
flag the same residues''). The two predictors disagree about which
residues are hard. Cross-model uncertainty does not collapse into one
shared signal on this RNA --- ensembling-based uncertainty and
confidence-head-based uncertainty are complementary, not redundant,
sources of information.
\end{enumerate}

bottom panel, the two per-residue uncertainty signals on independent

AF3's error spans 5--40~\AA{}.

\section{Discussion}

\subsection{The parsimonious geometric explanation}

The strongest competing explanation for our observation is the
following. The published Skilandat~2016 model has position 30 sitting
in the P1.1 segment, which is \emph{geometrically} in contact with
the P1 helix endpoints (residues 9 and 60). A correctly trained
structure predictor that learns base-pair contacts and 3D proximity
\emph{should} propagate a perturbation at any position to its 3D
neighbours. Therefore the cascade we observe could simply be ``DRfold2
correctly models the geometry of the P1.1$\leftrightarrow$P1 contact,''
which is a baseline expectation for any good model, not a
``mechanistic insight''.

This caveat does \emph{not} invalidate the observation. It does mean
the most defensible reading is:

\begin{quote}\itshape
The empirical cascade pattern is consistent with what one would
expect from a structurally well-calibrated predictor on this pair.
It is also consistent with a deeper claim that the model has captured
mechanism, but our experimental design cannot distinguish the two
explanations.
\end{quote}

\subsection{Relevance to NLP transformer interpretability}

Methodologically, micro-perturbation probing is a familiar
\emph{behavioral probing} technique in NLP: change a single token,
observe whether the model's distribution shifts in a
linguistically-coherent way. Our protocol is the structure-prediction
analogue. The pedagogical value is concrete: the input is unambiguous
(a 1-nt swap), the prediction is high-dimensional (3D coordinates),
and the expected response can be evaluated against an independent
ground truth (the biochemical mispairing model). We make no claim of
methodological novelty for the probing approach itself; we report a
single case study.

\subsection{What would constitute a real discovery}

\begin{itemize}
\item Predicting the activity \emph{ratio} (not just the structural
delta) from sequence alone, and showing it across many variant pairs.
\item Identifying a mutation/family where a predictor places a
cascade at residues \emph{not yet} described biochemically, then
validating those residues experimentally.
\item Showing the cascade pattern correlates with predicted confidence
(pLDDT-like score) in a way that reveals where models are reliable.
\item A multi-predictor benchmark in each model's intended input
regime, with MSA-driven runs of AlphaFold~3 and RhoFold+ where the
relevant alignments exist.
\end{itemize}

We did the third bullet partially (Experiment \#2, n=20 random null
controls, $p_{\mathrm{emp}} = 0.048$). We did the fourth partially
(Experiment \#3, an AF3 Server run, which reported pLDDT~$\sim$25 and
therefore did not yield a usable comparison). The protocol stays
offered as scaffolding; the cross-predictor benchmark in particular is
left open as the most promising follow-up.

\subsection{Limitations}

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Single RNA family.} All experiments are on a single 69-nt
ribozyme pair (R1107/R1108) and its peripheral controls. The
calibration correlations in \S\ref{sec:calib}, the AF3-vs-DRfold2
ground-truth gap in \S\ref{sec:gt}, and the cascade observation
are case-study evidence on one family. We do not claim
generalisation to other RNA classes without a multi-family
replication.
\item \textbf{Training-data exposure is asymmetric.} 7QR3 was deposited
in December~2021, after AlphaFold~3's declared September~2021 PDB
cutoff but well before DRfold2's 2025 training. Part of DRfold2's
3.73~\AA{} headline figure may reflect memorisation of this
structure, and not all of the DRfold2-vs-AF3 RMSD gap should be
attributed to architecture.
\item \textbf{Multi-chain AF3 submission.} The seven AF3 jobs were
submitted as a single multi-chain Server job for quota efficiency.
Per-residue pLDDT is intra-chain and Kabsch alignment is
translation-invariant, so the multi-chain framing should not affect
per-chain fold quality, but a per-chain re-submission would close
this caveat at the cost of one extra Server job.
\item \textbf{Top-5 threshold is heuristic.} The ``cascade'' metric
uses a fixed top-5 cut and a ``both anchors in top-5'' indicator. We
did not formalise sensitivity to either choice.
\item \textbf{Empirical p-value upper bound.} The
$p_{\mathrm{emp}} = 0.048$ from $n=20$ random NULLs (\S\ref{sec:n20})
uses the conservative add-1-smoothing upper bound, not a calibrated
permutation test against the underlying $\binom{69}{1}{\times}3$
alternative space.
\item \textbf{Calibration analysis at low absolute confidence.}
AF3's per-residue pLDDT ranks correctly against per-residue crystal
error, but in absolute terms the AF3 pLDDT values on this RNA
(\textit{ca.}~25) are below AlphaFold~3's standard ``low confidence''
threshold of 50. The calibration we report is therefore a
\emph{rank-order} property of pLDDT, not a calibration of the
absolute pLDDT-to-error mapping.
\item \textbf{No experimental kinetics.} The activity gap is taken from
Skilandat~2016~\cite{skilandat2016}; no cleavage assays were performed
in this study.
\end{itemize}

\section{Conclusion}

We report a small, honest empirical observation: a recent
single-sequence RNA structure predictor responds to the biologically
functional CPEB3 single-nucleotide variant by placing its largest
predicted structural changes at the published P1/P1.1 mechanistic
anchor, while five peripheral control mutations elicit local responses
only. We are explicit that this is consistent with --- but does not
require --- the predictor having ``learned mechanism''; a parsimonious
geometric explanation suffices. The contribution we stand behind is
the protocol, not a biological claim. The work is presented as
scaffolding for a properly powered cross-predictor study.
